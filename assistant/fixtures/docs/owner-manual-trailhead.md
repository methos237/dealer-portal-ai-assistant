# Trailhead Owners Manual (2025)

## Identification

This manual covers the 2025 THOR Trailhead 21, a compact off-road travel trailer built for unpaved roads and unpowered campsites. The Trailhead is a single-model line; there is no slide-out and no propane furnace in the standard configuration, though the ThermaCore TC-20 furnace is a factory option.

The VIN is stamped on the A-frame tongue and printed on the certification label inside the entry door. The VIN prefix is 1THRT21. The eighth character is the model year code (S for 2025). The build date appears on the certification label as MM/YYYY.

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall length | 21 ft 6 in |
| Exterior width | 7 ft 6 in |
| Exterior height (with roof rack) | 9 ft 10 in |
| Dry weight | 3,650 lb |
| GVWR | 5,200 lb |
| Cargo capacity | 1,550 lb |
| Hitch | 2-5/16 inch ball, articulating off-road coupler |
| Ground clearance | 18 in at axle |

Target tongue weight is 10 to 12 percent of loaded weight, or 520 to 620 lb at GVWR. The articulating coupler allows 360 degree rotation and does not accept a standard weight distribution hitch; a friction sway control bar is not required at this weight.

## Off-road suspension and tire pressures

The Trailhead uses an independent trailing-arm suspension with twin shock absorbers per side and coil springs. Suspension travel is 8 inches. The trailing arm pivot bolts are torqued to 220 ft-lb and should be checked at 500 miles and every 12 months. Shock absorbers are a wear item and are covered for the first 12 months.

Tires are 265/70R17 all-terrain on 17 inch alloy wheels with a six-lug pattern. Lug nut torque is 140 ft-lb. A full-size spare is mounted on the rear door. Tire pressure depends on surface:

| Surface | Cold pressure |
|---|---|
| Highway | 45 psi |
| Gravel and washboard | 28 psi |
| Sand and mud | 20 psi |
| Rock, slow speed | 24 psi |

Never exceed 25 mph below 30 psi, and reinflate to 45 psi before returning to pavement. Running low pressure at highway speed overheats the sidewall. A 12V compressor outlet is provided on the tongue box for this purpose; it is fused at 30 amps.

The electric drum brakes are 12 inch units. Adjust them at 3,000 miles and every 12 months. Wheel bearings are sealed cartridge type and are replaced rather than repacked, at 24 months or 24,000 miles. The wheel bearing SKU is BRG-CART-17.

## Solar and lithium battery

The Trailhead is designed to run without shore power. The roof carries two 200 watt solar panels for 400 watts total, feeding a 40 amp MPPT charge controller in the tongue box. In full sun the array produces about 25 amps at 13.4 volts. Expect 1.6 to 2.0 kilowatt hours per day in summer.

The house battery is a 200 amp hour lithium iron phosphate unit, SKU BAT-LI-200, with an internal battery management system. It is mounted in the tongue box and weighs 52 lb. Unlike an AGM battery it can be discharged to 10 percent without damage. State of charge is shown on the tongue box display; resting voltage is a poor indicator for lithium, since the battery reads 13.2 volts from 90 percent down to 20 percent.

The BAT-LI-200 has a low-temperature charge cutoff at 32 degrees F. Below that the battery will discharge but will not accept a charge from solar or the tow vehicle. The built-in heating pad turns on automatically when the battery is below 40 degrees F and a charge source is present; it draws 3 amps. If the battery is stored below 32 degrees F for the winter, disconnect it at 50 to 60 percent charge using the disconnect switch in the tongue box.

Charging from the tow vehicle goes through a 30 amp DC-to-DC charger, not a direct connection, because the lithium battery would otherwise pull more current than the seven-pin wiring can carry. The DC-to-DC charger only operates when it senses the tow vehicle alternator above 13.2 volts.

An inverter, SKU INV-2000, is fitted as standard on the Trailhead and powers the two interior outlets and the outdoor kitchen outlet. At full 2,000 watt output it draws 170 amps and will run for about one hour on a full BAT-LI-200. The inverter has a standby draw of 0.8 amps; turn it off when not in use.

The 12V fuse panel is inside the entry door: 15 amp water pump, 15 amp interior lights, 20 amp refrigerator, 10 amp USB outlets, 15 amp exterior lights, 15 amp furnace (if fitted), 30 amp compressor outlet, 5 amp battery display.

## Water and outdoor kitchen

The fresh water tank holds 30 gallons and is mounted between the frame rails with a steel guard plate. The gray tank holds 22 gallons. There is no black tank; the toilet is a cassette type with a 5 gallon removable cartridge that empties at a dump station or a household toilet.

The water pump is a 12V diaphragm unit rated at 3.0 gallons per minute with a 55 psi cutoff. A 5 micron sediment filter on the pump inlet should be rinsed every 3 months and replaced every 12 months. The exterior shower is on the driver side rear.

Hot water comes from an on-demand propane heater rated at 42,000 BTU; there is no tank. It requires a minimum flow of 0.5 gallons per minute to light and shuts off after 20 minutes of continuous flow. The igniter is the same direct-spark unit used on THOR tank heaters, SKU WH-IGN-10. The heater draws water from the fresh tank only; it is not plumbed for city water.

The outdoor kitchen slides out of the passenger-side rear on ball-bearing rails rated for 150 lb. It includes a two-burner propane stove, a stainless sink fed from the fresh tank, and a 120V outlet from the INV-2000. Push the slide fully in until both latches click before towing. The propane supply is two 20 lb cylinders on the tongue with an automatic changeover regulator set to 11 inches water column.

To winterize: drain the fresh tank from the low-point valve, open the on-demand heater drain plugs (two, on the bottom of the unit), open all faucets, run RV antifreeze through the pump until pink appears at every fixture, and pour a cup into the sink and shower drains. One gallon of antifreeze is enough. Empty and rinse the cassette toilet.

## Roof rack load limits

The roof rack is a welded aluminum platform bolted to the roof structure at eight points. It carries the solar panels on the forward section, leaving a 4 ft by 6 ft cargo area at the rear.

| Condition | Limit |
|---|---|
| Dynamic load (while towing), cargo area only | 200 lb |
| Static load (parked), total including solar | 600 lb |
| Single point load | 150 lb |

The dynamic limit includes any rooftop tent. A rooftop tent with two occupants exceeds the dynamic limit and is only permitted when parked. Do not mount a tent forward of the cargo area over the solar panels. Cargo must be strapped to the rack rails at four points; do not strap to the solar panel frames. Check rack mounting bolts (torque 25 ft-lb) at 500 miles and every 12 months. The rear ladder is rated for 250 lb.

## Warranty

THOR warrants the Trailhead against defects in materials and workmanship for 36 months from the date of delivery to the first retail owner. Coverage includes the frame, suspension arms, body, roof rack structure, plumbing, electrical system, solar array, charge controller, inverter, appliances, and cabinetry.

Excluded are tires, brake linings, shock absorbers after 12 months, fuses, bulbs, sealant maintenance, and damage from overloading, exceeding roof rack limits, water crossings deeper than the 18 inch axle height, or towing at highway speed with tires below 30 psi. The BAT-LI-200 lithium battery carries its own 60 month warranty, which exceeds the trailer warranty; claims for the battery still go through the THOR dealer.

Claims are handled by authorized THOR dealers through the dealer portal. Repairs above 500 dollars need pre-authorization. The dealer must submit the claim within 30 days of the repair, and THOR pays approved claims within 45 days.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Battery not charging from solar, sunny day | Battery below 32 degrees F or controller fault | Wait for heating pad; check controller display for error |
| Battery not charging while towing | Tow vehicle alternator below 13.2 volts or DC-to-DC charger fuse | Check 40 amp fuse at charger; confirm seven-pin connection |
| Inverter shuts off under load | Battery below 10 percent or load over 2,000 watts | Charge battery; reduce load |
| Battery display blank | 5 amp fuse blown | Replace fuse in entry door panel |
| Water heater will not light | Flow below 0.5 gpm or igniter fault | Open faucet fully; check propane; check WH-IGN-10 |
| Water heater shuts off mid-shower | 20 minute timer | Turn faucet off and on to reset |
| Pump runs but no water | Empty tank or clogged sediment filter | Fill tank; rinse filter |
| Trailer bounces on washboard | Tire pressure too high | Drop to 28 psi; keep speed under 25 mph |
| Outdoor kitchen will not latch | Debris in rail or slide not fully in | Clean rails; push slide fully home |
| Furnace (if fitted) code F1 | Sail switch FRN-SAIL not closing | Charge battery; clear return air; clean or replace switch |
| Brakes weak after water crossing | Wet drums | Apply brakes lightly for 1/4 mile to dry |
