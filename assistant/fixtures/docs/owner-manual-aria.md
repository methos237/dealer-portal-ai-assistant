# Aria Owners Manual (2024)

## Introduction and model identification

This manual covers the 2024 THOR Aria 24 and Aria 28 travel trailers. Both models share the same frame design, appliance package, and electrical layout. They differ in overall length, tank capacities, and weight ratings. Keep this manual in the trailer. Warranty service must be performed by an authorized THOR dealer, and the dealer will ask for the full vehicle identification number (VIN) at every visit.

The VIN is stamped on the A-frame tongue on the driver side and printed on the federal certification label just inside the entry door. The first seven characters identify the model:

| Model | VIN prefix | Overall length | Dry weight | GVWR | Cargo capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aria 24 | 1THRA24 | 27 ft 4 in | 5,900 lb | 7,600 lb | 1,700 lb |
| Aria 28 | 1THRA28 | 31 ft 8 in | 7,100 lb | 9,200 lb | 2,100 lb |

The eighth character is the model year code (R for 2024). Characters nine through seventeen are the production sequence. Production plant and build date appear on the certification label as MM/YYYY. Service bulletins refer to build dates, so note yours on the inside cover of this manual.

Never load the trailer beyond its GVWR. Cargo capacity is reduced by the weight of any dealer-installed options, water in the tanks (8.3 lb per gallon), and propane (4.2 lb per gallon).

## Warranty

THOR warrants the Aria against defects in materials and workmanship for 36 months from the date of delivery to the first retail owner. The 36 month period begins on the delivery date recorded by the dealer on the warranty registration, not the purchase or build date.

### What is covered

The structural warranty covers the welded steel frame, aluminum wall framing, laminated sidewalls, roof structure, and floor decking. The component warranty covers THOR-installed appliances, plumbing, the 12V and 120V electrical systems, slide-out mechanisms, the awning, and interior cabinetry. Appliances are serviced through THOR dealers; the owner does not need to contact the appliance maker directly.

### What is excluded

The warranty does not cover tires, brake linings, light bulbs, fuses, sealant maintenance, or damage caused by overloading, improper hitching, water intrusion from neglected seals, freezing, or modifications. The AGM house battery (SKU BAT-AGM-100) carries a shorter 12 month warranty. Normal wear items such as slide-out wiper seals and awning fabric are covered for the first 12 months only.

### Claim process

All claims go through an authorized dealer. The owner brings the trailer to the dealer, the dealer diagnoses the fault and records the VIN, mileage, and delivery date, and then submits a claim through the THOR dealer portal. Repairs with a total cost above 500 dollars require pre-authorization from THOR before work begins. The dealer must submit the completed claim within 30 days of the repair date. THOR pays approved claims within 45 days. Claims filed after the 36 month period are rejected unless the fault was reported to the dealer before the period ended.

## Hitching and towing

The Aria uses a conventional ball hitch. Both models require a 2-5/16 inch hitch ball rated for at least the trailer GVWR. A 2 inch ball will appear to seat but will not latch correctly and can separate under load.

Target tongue weight is 10 to 12 percent of the loaded trailer weight. For a fully loaded Aria 24 this is roughly 760 to 910 lb; for the Aria 28 it is roughly 920 to 1,100 lb. Tongue weight below 10 percent causes sway. Weigh the loaded trailer at a certified scale after the first trip and adjust cargo placement.

A weight distribution hitch with integrated sway control is required for the Aria 28 and strongly recommended for the Aria 24. THOR does not specify a brand. Set the spring bars so the tow vehicle front axle returns to within 50 percent of its unhitched height change. Friction sway control bars must be released before backing.

Before every trip: check the coupler latch and safety pin, cross the safety chains under the coupler, plug in the seven-pin connector, test the running lights and brake lights, confirm the breakaway cable is attached to the tow vehicle and not to the hitch, and check tire pressure. The trailer tires are ST225/75R15 load range E on both models and run at 80 psi cold.

The electric brakes on all four wheels are controlled by a brake controller in the tow vehicle. Set the controller gain so a firm stop from 25 mph does not lock the trailer wheels. Adjust the brake shoes at 3,000 miles and every 12 months after that.

## Slide-out operation

The Aria 24 has one slide-out (living room). The Aria 28 has two: the living room slide and the rear bedroom slide. Each slide is driven by a 12V rack-and-pinion motor, SKU SLD-MTR-12, mounted under the floor at the inboard end of the room.

The slide switch is next to the entry door. Hold the switch until the room stops and the motor clicks off. Do not release early; the seals only compress fully at the end of travel. The trailer must be level and the stabilizer jacks lowered before extending a slide. Keep people and pets clear of the room during travel.

### Seal care

Each slide has an outer bulb seal that compresses against the room when retracted and a wiper seal that sweeps the roof of the room during travel. Clean both seals every 3 months with mild soap and water and treat with a dry silicone spray. Never use petroleum-based lubricants; they swell the rubber. Inspect the rear bedroom slide seal on the Aria 28 for cracks or loss of bulb shape at every 6 month service. A seal that stays flattened after the room is extended has lost its memory and should be replaced.

### Manual override

If the motor fails, the slide can be moved by hand. Turn off the 12V disconnect. Locate the override hex on the motor gearbox under the floor. Remove the drive pin and turn the hex with a 3/4 inch socket on a ratchet. It takes about 40 turns to retract a slide fully. Reinstall the drive pin after the room is closed. Do not tow with a slide partially extended.

## Water system

| Model | Fresh water | Gray water | Black water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aria 24 | 44 gal | 38 gal | 30 gal |
| Aria 28 | 52 gal | 46 gal | 38 gal |

The water pump is a 12V diaphragm pump rated at 3.0 gallons per minute with a 55 psi cutoff. City water enters through a pressure regulator; the inlet must not exceed 60 psi.

### Water heater

The water heater is a THOR AquaLine WH-10, a 10 gallon gas and electric unit mounted behind the exterior panel on the passenger side. The gas burner is 12,000 BTU; the electric element is 1,400 watts. Both can run at the same time for the fastest recovery. The gas side uses a direct-spark igniter, SKU WH-IGN-10, that fires three times before locking out. A lockout is indicated by a steady red light on the interior switch. Reset by turning the switch off for 10 seconds and back on. If the heater locks out three times in a row, the igniter electrode gap (which should be 1/8 inch) or the gas supply pressure (11 inches water column) is likely at fault.

Never run the electric element with the tank empty. The element burns out in under a minute without water.

### Winterizing

1. Drain the fresh tank using the low-point drain valve under the trailer.
2. Open the water heater bypass valve behind the heater and drain the heater through its anode rod port. Inspect the anode; replace it when less than 25 percent remains.
3. Open all faucets and the low-point hot and cold drains until flow stops.
4. Connect the winterizing hose from the pump to a jug of RV antifreeze and run the pump until each faucet, the toilet, and the outside shower discharge pink.
5. Pour one cup of antifreeze into each drain trap.
6. Close the bypass valve in the spring before refilling, or the heater will not fill.

Two gallons of antifreeze is enough for the Aria 24; the Aria 28 needs three.

## Electrical

### 12V system

House power comes from one Group 31 AGM battery, SKU BAT-AGM-100, rated at 100 amp hours. A second battery can be added in parallel on the tongue tray. The battery is charged by the converter when connected to shore power or by the tow vehicle through the seven-pin connector while driving. A battery disconnect switch is located in the pass-through storage. Turn it off during storage; the propane detector and stereo memory otherwise draw about 0.4 amps and will flatten the battery in about 10 days.

Battery state of charge can be estimated from resting voltage: 12.8 volts is full, 12.4 volts is about 75 percent, 12.0 volts is about 50 percent. Do not discharge the AGM battery below 12.0 volts.

### Converter and inverter

The converter is a 55 amp three-stage unit in the fuse panel cabinet. It puts out 14.4 volts in bulk mode, 13.6 volts in absorption, and 13.2 volts in float. A converter stuck in bulk mode for more than 24 hours will overcharge the battery.

An inverter, SKU INV-2000, is available as a dealer-installed option. It is a 2,000 watt pure sine wave unit that powers the two living-area outlets and the television when not on shore power. It draws up to 180 amps from the battery at full output, so a single BAT-AGM-100 will run it for less than 30 minutes at full load. THOR recommends two batteries when the INV-2000 is fitted.

### Fuse panel

The 12V fuse panel is under the refrigerator. Fuses are ATO blade type. Circuits: 15 amp furnace, 15 amp water pump, 10 amp water heater, 20 amp slide-out control, 15 amp interior lights, 10 amp propane detector and stereo, 30 amp awning, 15 amp tank heaters. The 120V panel has a 30 amp main breaker and 15 amp branch breakers for the converter, GFCI outlets, water heater element, and air conditioner (20 amp).

## Furnace and appliances

### Furnace

The furnace is a THOR ThermaCore TC-30 rated at 30,000 BTU. It is a ducted forced-air unit mounted under the dinette. It requires at least 10.5 volts to run; below that the blower slows and the sail switch will not close. The sail switch, SKU FRN-SAIL, is a paddle switch inside the blower housing that proves airflow before the gas valve opens. A dusty or bent sail switch is the most common cause of a furnace that blows air but never lights.

The furnace control board flashes a fault code on the red LED visible through the intake grille:

| Code | Meaning | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Sail switch did not close within 30 seconds | Low voltage, blocked return air, bent or dirty FRN-SAIL |
| F2 | High limit switch open | Blocked ducts, closed registers, failed limit switch |
| F3 | Ignition lockout after 3 attempts | Empty propane, air in line, fouled electrode |
| F4 | Flame sensed with gas valve closed | Faulty gas valve or board |
| F5 | Supply voltage below 10.5 volts | Discharged battery, corroded connector |

Reset any code by cycling the thermostat off for 5 minutes. Keep the furnace exhaust on the exterior wall clear of snow and debris.

### Refrigerator, range, and air conditioner

The refrigerator is a 10 cubic foot 12V compressor unit. It draws about 4 amps when running and should be started 8 hours before loading. The range has three burners and a 17 inch oven. The oven pilot is lit by hand; hold the knob in for 30 seconds after the pilot lights. The roof air conditioner is 13,500 BTU and requires shore power or a 3,000 watt generator; it will not run from the INV-2000.

## Awning

The power awning uses a 12V motor, SKU AWN-1200, in the roller tube. The switch is inside the entry door. Extend the awning fully; a partially extended awning flaps more in wind. Lower one end by 6 inches with the arm adjuster so rain runs off.

Retract the awning whenever wind exceeds 20 mph or when leaving the trailer. Gusts above 30 mph can bend the arms even when the awning is retracted only partway. Wind damage is not covered by warranty. In the event of motor failure, remove the plastic cap on the driver-side arm and turn the manual crank hex with a 7/16 inch socket. Clean the fabric twice a year with a soft brush and diluted dish soap; allow it to dry before rolling.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing works on 12V | Battery disconnect off or battery below 10 volts | Turn on disconnect; charge battery; check 30 amp main fuse |
| Slide-out will not move, motor hums | Trailer not level or room binding | Level trailer; check for obstruction; use manual override if needed |
| Slide-out moves in one direction only | Failed switch or motor relay | Dealer to test switch and replace SLD-MTR-12 if motor fails one direction |
| Water pump runs continuously | Air leak on suction side or empty tank | Fill tank; check pump strainer and fittings |
| Water heater red light on | Igniter lockout | Reset switch; check propane; check WH-IGN-10 gap |
| Furnace blows but no heat, code F1 | Sail switch not closing | Charge battery; clear return air; clean or replace FRN-SAIL |
| Furnace cycles on and off, code F2 | Restricted airflow | Open all registers; check duct connections |
| Awning will not retract | Motor stalled or fuse blown | Check 30 amp fuse; use manual crank; dealer to replace AWN-1200 |
| Trailer sways at speed | Tongue weight below 10 percent | Move cargo forward; verify weight distribution setup |
| Refrigerator not cooling, fan runs | Low voltage or blocked condenser | Check battery voltage above 12.0; clear exterior vent |
| GFCI outlets dead | Tripped GFCI | Reset GFCI in bathroom; it feeds all exterior and kitchen outlets |
