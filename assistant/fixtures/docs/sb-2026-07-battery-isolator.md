# SB-2026-07 Battery isolator relay replacement

## Subject

Battery isolator relay, SKU ISO-RLY-200, on 2024 Summit 32 and Summit 36 motorhomes: diagnosis and replacement of relays that stick in the closed position. Issued 2026-07-08. Supersedes none.

## Affected units

This bulletin applies to all 2024 Summit 32 (VIN prefix 1THRS32) and Summit 36 (VIN prefix 1THRS36) motorhomes with a certification label build date between 01/2024 and 10/2024 inclusive. Units built from 11/2024 received the revised relay described below at the factory and are not affected.

## Condition

Owners report chassis batteries too low to crank the engine after one or two nights parked with house loads running, even though the house batteries remain charged. Some owners report the BATTERY BOOST switch has no effect because the banks are already connected. Investigation shows the original ISO-RLY-200 contacts can weld closed after repeated engagement under load, typically after 200 to 400 boost or engine-start cycles. Once welded closed, the relay connects the chassis and house banks permanently, and house loads such as the refrigerator, inverter standby, and furnace drain the chassis batteries.

The revised relay carries the same SKU, ISO-RLY-200, with a date code of 2610 or later stamped on the case. It has silver-alloy contacts and a 250 amp continuous rating. It is a direct replacement using the same mounting holes and terminals.

## Diagnostic voltage checks

Perform these checks before replacing the relay. Use a digital voltmeter on the DC volts range.

1. With the engine off, BATTERY BOOST released, and shore power disconnected for at least 30 minutes, measure the chassis battery voltage at the chassis battery positive post and the house battery voltage at the house bank positive post. Healthy, independent banks will read different values. If both banks read within 0.05 volts of each other, the relay is likely closed.
2. Turn on the interior lights and the water pump, then measure the chassis battery voltage again. If the chassis voltage drops in step with the house voltage, the banks are connected and the relay is stuck closed.
3. Measure the relay coil terminal (small terminal marked C) to ground. With the ignition off and boost released it must read 0 volts. If it reads 12 volts, the relay is being commanded closed by a wiring fault, not a stuck contact, and the relay should not be replaced; check the boost switch and ignition feed instead.
4. Measure across the two large relay terminals. With the coil at 0 volts a healthy relay reads full battery voltage difference between the banks (typically 0.2 to 1.5 volts). A reading below 0.02 volts with the coil at 0 volts confirms welded contacts.
5. Start the engine and confirm the coil terminal reads above 12 volts and both banks read within 0.3 volts of each other. If the banks are not within 0.3 volts with the engine running, the relay is stuck open or the 200 amp fuse at the relay is blown.

Replace the relay only if step 4 confirms welded contacts or step 5 confirms a stuck-open relay with a good fuse.

## Replacement steps

1. Turn off the house battery disconnect and disconnect the negative cable from the chassis battery bank. Wait 2 minutes for the inverter capacitors to discharge.
2. Open the battery compartment behind the driver-side front wheel. The relay is on the rear wall next to the 200 amp fuse.
3. Label the two large cables and the small coil wire. Remove the cable nuts with a 1/2 inch socket and the coil nut with a 5/16 inch socket.
4. Remove the two mounting bolts with a 3/8 inch socket and lift out the old relay. Record its date code on the claim.
5. Mount the new relay with the terminals oriented the same way and torque the mounting bolts to 8 ft-lb.
6. Reconnect the large cables and torque the terminal nuts to 10 ft-lb. Reconnect the coil wire and torque to 20 in-lb. Apply dielectric grease to all terminals.
7. Inspect the 200 amp fuse and replace it if there is any discoloration.
8. Reconnect the chassis negative cable and turn on the house disconnect.
9. Repeat diagnostic steps 1, 3, and 5 to confirm the banks are isolated with the engine off and connected with the engine running.
10. Charge the chassis batteries fully before returning the coach. Chassis batteries drained below 11.5 volts more than twice may not recover and should be load tested; chassis batteries are not covered under this bulletin.
